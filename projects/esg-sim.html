— THE PROBLEM

ESG sounds virtuous. Most beginners don't know what it costs.

Sustainable investing is increasingly the default story we tell new investors — but the trade-offs (long horizons, fund selection, sector exclusions, return variance) rarely make it into the narrative. The result: people opt in to ESG without understanding what they're opting out of.

We wanted to build something that put the trade-offs in the learner's hands — let them make portfolio choices, see simulated consequences over time, and feel the shape of ESG investing rather than read about it.

— MY ROLE

Product strategy and UX, end to end.

  • Defined the product vision: an interactive simulation, not a course or a quiz.
  • Designed the core game loop — pick a portfolio, advance the market clock, see outcomes, iterate.
  • Built the data model for investment scenarios: asset classes, ESG ratings, market events, volatility ranges.
  • Drove UX design across onboarding, portfolio building, simulation playback, and reflection.
  • Validated product-market fit using simulated market data and playtests with first-time investor personas.

— APPROACH

Four moves, four feelings.

Onboard

Strip the financial jargon. Anchor the player with a story and a starting bankroll.

Choose

Portfolio building with ESG-tagged assets. Visual contrast between sustainable and conventional choices, without moralizing.

Simulate

Time advances. Markets move based on the data model. Players watch their portfolio's value (and ESG impact) shift.

Reflect

Post-round breakdown: what worked, what didn't, what ESG choices cost or earned. Then play again with better intuition.

— OUTCOMES

Recognition and lessons.

  • Top 4 in Singapore at the Goldman Sachs Engineering Engage Hackathon — recognized for product strategy and execution.
  • A working simulation that put ESG trade-offs in a learner's hands, not a textbook.
  • Validated approach — simulated market data was enough to demonstrate PMF without needing live financial integrations.
  • First taste of product leadership — defining the why, the what, and the feel of a product end-to-end while still in undergrad.
