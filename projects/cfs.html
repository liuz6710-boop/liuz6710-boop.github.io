— THE PROBLEM

Operations live in spreadsheets, customers live in emails.

Container Freight Stations sit between port operations and shipper / consignee logistics. PSA's existing tooling was fragmented across legacy systems and ad-hoc workflows — every new customer integration was a one-off, every billing question was a CSV pull, and operational bottlenecks only surfaced after they'd already cost the day's throughput.

We needed a single B2B platform that could track every container through Import, Transshipment, and Export flows; plan loading operations; handle billing across customer types; and speak natively with the rest of the port ecosystem — community, warehouse management and downstream logistics systems — and with each customer's own IT stack.

— INTEGRATION ARCHITECTURE

Two protocols, one platform.

Most customers arrive in one of two camps. Modern API-first IT stacks expect real-time JSON; legacy enterprise customers — common in port, chemical, and pharmaceutical logistics — still run on XML EDI files dropped on a schedule. The platform speaks both natively, and every new integration starts with picking the right one.

REST / JSON API

For modern customer IT — real-time, webhook-driven, idempotent. Sync vs. async, polling vs. push. The right choice is the one the customer's team can actually operate at 3am.

EDI (XML)

For legacy enterprise — file-based, batch-scheduled, predictable cadence. Field-level mapping against the customer's existing EDI taxonomy; failure modes documented up front.

Contract & Mapping

Schemas, error codes, retry semantics, idempotency keys, customer-field → platform-field translation. The boring documents that prevent most post-launch integration issues.

UAT

Test fixtures, staging walkthroughs, go-live criteria. Customers see the system work on their own data before signing off, not after.

— DELIVERY & COORDINATION

Shipping is a team sport.

A clean API contract doesn't ship a platform. Getting from "signed spec" to "live in production" is the work — and most of it is people, not code.

Cross-team go-live

Coordinated rollout across Operations, Finance, Engineering, Customer Success, and customer IT. Surface dependencies early, decide what blocks launch, prepare a rollback plan before anyone needs it.

Vendor coordination

Align timelines, escalation paths, and integration ownership with external IT vendors and customer-side integrators. Keep the contract honest and the meeting cadence boring.

Change management

Walk operations teams through new workflows. Document what changes for whom. Make sure the people doing the work understand the system before it goes live.

Post-launch support

Runbooks, monitoring, escalation paths. The first two weeks after go-live are where most platforms either prove themselves or get rolled back.

— OUTCOMES

What shipped.

  • First release in ~2 months — PSA's first digital platform for CFS operations, replacing decentralised spreadsheet workflows; later scaled to an enterprise logistics client handling ~1,500 containers per month.
  • 8 enterprise clients delivered, 4 of them needing custom integrations — discovery, UAT and go-live run end to end, then training so the teams actually used the system.
  • One reusable billing framework replacing per-client pricing logic — ~3 staff-hours/day per client of manual invoice preparation removed and billing errors down ~20%.
  • A core data model that holds up — tracking inventory below the customer-order level so partial processing and returns stay accurate in both stock and charges.
  • A silent data bug caught before anyone noticed — diagnosed a cross-system consistency failure and designed the compensating rollback mechanism that prevented recurrence.
  • Smart picking algorithm for pharmaceutical logistics clients — 70% picking efficiency improvement. See case study →
  • S$1.5M+ projected 5-year revenue — sized from merchant behaviour analysis and presented to leadership, which won the initiative its slot against competing projects.
  • KPI dashboards for platform adoption, transaction volume, and operational throughput — bottlenecks that used to surface in post-mortems now surface in the backlog.
